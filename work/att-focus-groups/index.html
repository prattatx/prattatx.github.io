Validating a discovery concept before anyone built it

Before engineering built a new discovery concept, we tested it with analysts. Task-based focus groups against an interactive prototype gave the team comparable feedback on every flow.

Role
Research lead within the Chief Data Office (AI User Experience). Directed the study and set its protocol, task flows and prototype-driven test plan with the team.
Context
Chief Data Office of a Fortune 10 telecom, validating a data and analytics discovery concept before build.
Kind of work
Research
Date
2019

The problem

A new analytics discovery concept needed validation with real users before engineering invested in building it. The team needed evidence that the core flows, finding the right people, finding the right data, and searching effectively, matched how analysts actually think and work.

The approach

We ran structured, task-based focus groups against an interactive prototype. The protocol walked participants through a defined set of flows.

  • Reviewing and orienting on the home page.
  • Selecting a subject-matter-expert card and reaching the detail view.
  • Selecting a data card and reaching the detail view.
  • Searching from the home page, including filters and type-ahead suggestion, through to results and a detail page.

Each flow had a fixed sequence of prototype screens, so feedback was comparable across participants and tied to a concrete interaction.

The outcome

Comparable, flow-level feedback on a concept before build, letting the team refine the prototype and de-risk the engineering investment. The protocol turned subjective reactions into structured signal mapped to specific screens and tasks.

Leading the team

  • Put the concept in front of analysts before engineering committed to building it.
  • Fixed the flows and the screen sequence, so feedback from every group could be compared.
  • Gave the product team findings mapped to specific screens and tasks, which they used to refine the prototype.

Method-only. Proprietary tool names, real participant names and IDs, and internal data removed. No confidential employer material reproduced.
