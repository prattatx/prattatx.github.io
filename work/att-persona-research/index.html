North Star: customer-centric design for enterprise analytics

A mixed-method research program I led inside a Fortune 10 Chief Data Office. Contextual inquiry, cognitive task analysis and a validating survey produced personas that leadership used to set priorities.

Role
Program lead within the Chief Data Office (AI User Experience). I set the research plan and methods, directed the work, and turned the findings into product direction for leadership.
Context
Chief Data Office of a Fortune 10 telecom, designing service and self-service analytics systems for internal data analysts.
Kind of work
Research
Date
2018 to 2019

The problem

The organization wanted data-powered decision-making across a 200,000-person company, but the analytics tools its own data analysts used carried friction that slowed adoption. Leadership needed product decisions grounded in how analysts actually worked, to raise effectiveness without taxing the efficiency of skilled users.

The approach

I wrote a formal User Experience Research Plan that applied human-factors engineering to persona development. We then used those personas to drive design.

  • Contextual inquiry, observing analysts doing real work in their existing tool suite.
  • Cognitive task analysis to locate pain points and the drivers of productivity.
  • Empathy mapping (think, feel, say, do) to build a shared, human picture of the user.
  • Training-needs and tool-gap analysis to separate capability gaps from design gaps.
  • UI analysis to surface usability issues in current and proposed systems.
  • Survey research to quantify and validate the qualitative signal at scale.

Findings rolled up into a customer-centric design approach: personas, objectives, and a vision-casting frame that gave the organization a common reference for prioritization.

The outcome

A validated persona set and a customer-centric design framework that grounded analytics-product decisions in real analyst behavior. Leadership got a defensible basis for prioritizing friction reduction where it mattered most, in service of enabling data-driven decisions company-wide.

How I led it

  • I set the research plan and chose the six methods.
  • I paired the qualitative work with a survey, so the personas were validated at scale.
  • I tied the findings to business objectives (adoption and effectiveness) and took them to leadership as a basis for priorities.

Method-only. Proprietary tool names, real persona content, and internal data removed. No confidential employer material reproduced. Consolidates the North Star customer-centric design program and its persona-research workstream, which were one body of work.
