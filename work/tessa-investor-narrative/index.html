Making a new category legible to investors

As CEO I owned the story the company told investors. One protagonist, then the market, then the wedge, with every claim sourced or labeled as an assumption.

Role
Founder and CEO. I set the narrative, wrote and designed the deck, and revised it against investor feedback.
Context
Pre-seed ambient-AI consumer hardware and software startup. My own company.
Kind of work
Strategy, Writing
Date
2025 to 2026, iterated across versions

The problem

A novel product in an unfamiliar category had to be made instantly legible to investors: what it is, why now, who buys it, and why it wins. Roughly fifteen slides, without drowning the story in features.

The approach

I sequenced the deck so that each slide did one job.

  • Problem told through a single named protagonist, so the pain lands before it is sized.
  • Scale and why-now to turn the personal story into a market.
  • Solution framed as quantified outcomes: what changes for the user.
  • Traction, how it works, market, model, go-to-market in an order that answers the next question an investor would ask, in the order they ask it.
  • Competition as a positioning table that makes the wedge clear at a glance.
  • Team, roadmap, and the ask to close on credibility and a concrete next milestone.

Every claim was tied to a cited source or labeled as an assumption, so the deck held up under diligence.

The outcome

A tight, diligence-ready investor narrative that distills a complex ambient-AI product into a legible, sequenced story with a clear wedge and a concrete milestone. Iterated across multiple versions against investor feedback.

How I led it

  • I decided what the company would claim, and what it would label as an assumption.
  • I ordered the deck around the questions investors ask, in the order they ask them.
  • I revised the narrative across versions as investor feedback came in.

Method-only. Traction, financials, raise terms, and team specifics removed. Focus is the narrative architecture and the craft.
