Turning a founder's invention into a patent pipeline

A one-day workshop I designed and led for an early-stage hardware startup. Product dissection, use-journey mapping and my 10x ideation method, scored down to a shortlist for patent counsel.

Role
Designed the workshop and led the day. I set the agenda, ran the strategy and ideation segments, and led the colleagues who co-facilitated the other segments.
Context
An early-stage hardware startup in the balance and movement-training space. Client unnamed.
Kind of work
Workshop, Strategy
Date
June 2025

The problem

A founder with deep domain expertise and a shippable product had real invention in hand but no structured way to convert it into a defensible patent portfolio. The goal was to surface novel, non-obvious, patentable concepts, prioritize them, and give patent counsel clear direction.

The approach

I designed a single day that moved from strategy to a prioritized shortlist ready for counsel.

  • Why patents. Facilitated discussion to pin down what the portfolio was actually for: licensing, defensibility, visibility, and how aggressively to file.
  • Product dissection. Deconstructed the existing product into features, mechanisms, and materials, the raw surface area where claims live.
  • Customer-use journey mapping. Mapped diverse use cases across markets to expose where new, ownable utility could emerge.
  • 10x ideas. My own facilitation method: push participants to imagine the product ten times bigger, not ten percent better, across three prompt categories (extreme use cases, exponential technology jumps, unexpected user mashups), favoring quantity and non-obviousness over feasibility.
  • Sort, score, prioritize. Affinity-grouped the ideas, filtered each against a patent test (novel, useful, non-obvious, describable, strategically valuable), then dot-voted to a shortlist.

The outcome

A prioritized shortlist of patentable concepts with a clear filing direction, including provisional strategy, that fed directly to patent counsel. Ad-hoc filing replaced with a deliberate, founder-aligned portfolio plan.

How I led it

  • I set the agenda and the method, and led a group of co-facilitators through the day.
  • I held every idea to what the founder wanted the portfolio to do: licensing, defensibility and visibility.
  • I closed the day with a decision: a scored shortlist and a filing direction that went to patent counsel.
  • The method draws on my own record as a named inventor on 358 granted U.S. patents.

The method

The 10x Ideas facilitation guide (three prompt categories, timed divergent rounds, affinity sorting, patent-filter voting) is my own method, which is why it can be described here. The client's session content is theirs and is not reproduced.

Method-only. Client unnamed. The client's specific inventions, scoring frameworks, and filings are withheld. The facilitation method described is my own.
