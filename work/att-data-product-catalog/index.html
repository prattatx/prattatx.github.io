Work / Case study / Product and UI design / 2021

Making enterprise data findable, traceable, and reusable

A data product catalog for a Fortune 10 Chief Data Office. I led a team of engineers and leaders to one design that put search, lineage and data flows in a single interface, for web and mobile.

Role
Product and UX design lead within the Chief Data Office (AI User Experience). I brought a multidisciplinary team of engineers and leaders to a shared design, using human-factors methods to get there.
Context
Chief Data Office of a Fortune 10 telecom, building self-service data findability for internal analysts and data scientists.
Kind of work
Product design
Date
2021

The problem

In a large enterprise, analysts and data scientists struggled to find, understand, and reuse data products across business units. Metadata lived in one place, the data in another, lineage somewhere else, so people fell back on emailing data owners or asking their team. The result was duplicated effort and slow time-to-insight.

The approach

I led a product and UX design effort to make data discoverable and reusable through a single catalog, designed for both web and mobile.

  • A browseable, searchable catalog of data products with type-ahead and filtering.
  • Visible product lineage and data flows, so users could trust and trace what they found.
  • Detail views connecting metadata to access paths, collapsing the hop between learning about a data product and using it.
  • Designed across web and mobile so the experience held up wherever analysts worked.

The work was grounded in the same human-factors research that produced the persona and customer-centric design program. Design decisions traced back to how analysts actually search and decide.

The outcome

A concrete, designed catalog concept that turned scattered data-discovery behavior into a single coherent interface, giving engineering a clear target and leadership a tangible vision for self-service data findability.

How I led it

  • I brought engineers and leaders who each held part of the problem to one shared design.
  • I grounded the design in the research from the persona program: how analysts search and decide.
  • I gave engineering a clear target and leadership a concrete picture of self-service data findability.
